This guide follows the live gameplay path from [`main.tscn`](../../main.tscn) and [`main.gd`](../../main.gd). The project targets Godot 4.7 with Forward+ and physics interpolation. ## Player input, movement, and aiming

- [`project.godot`](../../project.godot) registers `attack` on LMB and `block` on RMB. Main supplies the keyboard defaults through its binding setup. The defaults include WASD, Shift sprint, Space jump, E interact, F1 Training Ground roster, and Q cards.
- Player `_input()` captures press and release edges even when both fit between physics ticks. The physics loop advances the attack, buffer, movement, and resource clocks. A short tap requests a light attack. Holding enters charge and full charge releases automatically. Releasing an incomplete charge converts to the light move for that stage if it can start. [Charge return](../../player/charge_return.gd) handles the visual return when that move is unavailable.
- Guard starts while RMB is held and stamina meets the minimum. Raising guard can cancel an unreleased strike. A light input during guard lowers it for the attack. The ordinary guard path resumes while RMB remains held and guard is available. [Stamina rules](../../data/stamina_rules.tres) own the resource costs and the recharge delays after contact, release, and a break. Shields change cost and pose. Sustained hazards and damage-over-time ticks have their own damage paths, and marked unblockable impacts bypass guard.
- Movement uses CharacterBody3D physics, including acceleration, grounded jump buffer and grace, knockback, and water transitions. `warning_walk_profile()` exposes the slow combat walking speed and acceleration used to calculate ground-warning escape time. Changes to combat strafe or backpedal speed need a warning escape check.
- On the ground, the two-handed mace briefly raises held movement speed to a target of `1.40×`. A light braces during its opening, and a heavy eases out of the charge slowdown as it releases. Both use the gameplay strike clock. The surge peaks through `Impact` and fades by `Trace End`. A hard stop ends the boost immediately. Character acceleration still controls actual speed, and the live body position drives the next trace sample. Check a moving light, both charged strafe directions, a stationary charge, and a close pillar stop in [combat input](../../tests/player_combat_input.gd). The peak is editable in [Workbench](workbench-and-data.md#forms-and-inspection).
- [First-person camera effects](../../player/effects/camera/first_person_camera_effects.gd) follow the interpolated body translation while applying the latest aim and visual roll. Combat calls `get_aim_transform()` for the live eye transform with locomotion roll. Render-only damage roll, impact shake, and lens feedback stay outside collision aim. Use the camera's explicit pose synchronization when moving the body or updating look. Preserve interpolation resets at teleports.
- [Weapon sway](../../player/weapon_sway/weapon_sway.gd) owns procedural held movement with the [default motion resource](../../player/weapon_sway/default_weapon_motion.tres) and the weapon's motion style. Pose, locomotion sway, and physical trace sampling meet at the weapon view. A viewmodel change should preserve the weapon grip, both wrists on two-handers, and center-screen visibility.
- The weapon gait advances by resolved horizontal ground distance after platform motion is removed. The resolved sprint state blends between the walking and sprinting step shapes and distances. Speed scales the bob amplitude independently. A step boundary also triggers the player's existing walk or sprint sound. The gait phase holds while stationary, airborne, or swimming. In Forward+, compare walking, starting a sprint, and stopping against the ground, then check that wall contact and jumping stop the step rhythm.

## Two-handed mace guard

The [two-handed mace AnimationPlayer](../../player/weapons/mace_2h.tscn) keys a raise into a planted two-hand brace (`guard_raise` → `guard`). Guard protects as soon as the input succeeds. On an absorbed incoming hit, [the player block resolver](../../player/player.gd) signals [the weapon view](../../player/weapon_view.gd) to play `guard_hit` from the current visible pose. The recoil settles into the held brace. The last absorbed hit before depletion plays `guard_break`, which drops the weapon back to ready. The brace and its reactions use the same wrist anchors and two-arm rig as the mace swings. Directional guard camera kick still comes from [CameraFeedback](../../player/effects/camera/camera_feedback.gd).

To tune those clips, open the mace scene's AnimationPlayer. Keep the raise endpoint and both recoil joins aligned with `guard`, and the break endpoint aligned with `idle`. Keep the `Recoil Peak` marker at the main kick in `guard_hit`. A second hit during that reaction blends from the visible pose toward this marker, so the mace can take another hit without first moving back out to the brace. In the Training Ground, equip a Quarry Maul, hold RMB against a melee enemy, and check the raise, recoil, and settle. Let Stamina run out to see the break. Release RMB during recoil to check the blend back to ready. [Player combat input](../../tests/player_combat_input.gd) also checks the Stamina, Life, aim, and unblockable paths.

## Player strikes and authored timelines

1. `cast_ability()` chooses the appropriate ability, hand, chain stage, and clip. An attack's clock uses the actual clip length and [weapon commitment timing](../../combat/weapons/weapon_roles.gd) when markers are available. [Attack tempo](../../data/attack_tempo.tres) caps the rate after item attack speed and temporary haste combine. Charge uses the selected charge clip and the player scene's minimum hold duration. The same clock drives the pose and hit and buffer windows.
2. Edit the AnimationPlayer in the weapon family scene. A driven attack needs `Trace Start`, `Trace End`, and `Input Buffer`. `Impact` is optional and defaults to the middle of the trace window. Mace lights can also key `Combo Entry` before `Trace Start`, `Combo Exit` after `Trace End`, and `Terrain Start` for the first terrain sample. Keep `Terrain Start` between `Trace Start` and `Impact`. When it is absent, terrain can register from `Trace Start`. Missing or invalid trace markers take the fallback path. [The Workbench timing helper](../../addons/hearth_workbench/workbench_weapon_timing.gd) exposes the markers.
3. `_advance_driven_strike()` maps elapsed clock time into the clip. Melee commits at `Trace Start`, samples through `Trace End`, and finishes the strike after the active window. A driven staff attack releases at `Impact`. For most weapons, `Input Buffer` opens follow-up buffering. For mace lights, it gates the start of the next attack. The ability cooldown and mana spend commit once through `AbilityRuntime`.
4. [Weapon view](../../player/weapon_view.gd) samples `DamageRoot` and `DamageTip`, or the weapon's `TraceRoot` and `TraceTip` when it uses one guide pair. The box keeps the weapon pose's roll. [Melee sweep](../../player/melee_sweep.gd) casts the previous shaft, the turning box, and the translating box, including initial overlap. The fixed attack cone and cover rays use gameplay eye and weapon points. A closure sample takes the window through `Trace End`. The existing parameterized volume remains the fallback for missing guides or animation markers.
5. Actual contacts retain the striking hand. The paired charged lunge gives each weapon its own sweep, per-actor record, and cleave budget. Hand identity selects the damage, attribute, and legendary effects. [Weapon balance](../../data/player_weapon_balance.tres) owns ordinary multi-target and staff fork scaling.
6. In **Training Ground → F1 → Weaponry**, equip the spawned weapon and enable **Show Player Weapon Traces** to see the cast endpoints and the mace's compact terrain box. **Contact Preset** switches between Training, Carry Through, and Refuge. Use the **Enemy** tab to match player level, target level, and affixes. [Training Ground controls](ui-and-inventory.md#controls-and-item-lifecycle)

The standard two-handed mace combo in the Training Ground is left → right → down-left → down-right. Tap LMB again within the combo window to loop from left. Wait out the window to reset to left. Hold **A** as you start holding LMB for a leftward heavy, or **D** for a rightward heavy. Verify the selected side stays latched when you let go of strafe during the load. At normal attack speed, a sustained charge releases at about 1.5 seconds from button-down. Release at about half a second for a manual heavy. The charge's `Heavy Ready` marker owns that threshold. An earlier button-up can blend into the lateral light for the latched side before its trace starts, or return to ready when the light is unavailable. A committed heavy sets the next tap to the opposite lateral side.

The [two-handed mace scene](../../player/weapons/mace_2h.tscn) uses the close ready pose as `idle` and as the scene's rest and reset pose. The Training Ground uses that pose with procedural sway. Its matching attacks are `light_left`, `light_right`, `light_down_left`, and `light_down_right`, plus `charge_left` and `heavy_left` or `charge_right` and `heavy_right`. The player selects the clip at cast start and uses its markers for the pose and trace.

The rigid weapon `Pose` draws away from the direction of the heavy strike. Each held charge ends at its release's first pose. The [preview arm rig](../../player/weapons/preview_two_hand_rig.gd) reads `PreviewArmRig` keys to rotate the upper body, solves both arms to the wrist markers, and drives the existing sleeves. Check the ready crop, the windup silhouette on both sides, both hand contacts, the Quarry Maul's leading narrow face, and the return to idle in Forward+. Enable **Show Player Weapon Traces** to inspect the active sweep.

The lateral lights chamber the head close to the eye with the shaft angled back. The head reaches forward through a lower center crossing, then the hands retract on the far side. The two downswings lift the head beside the camera and drive it forward and down across the aim line. Their camera keys pitch down and bank toward the cut. Check all four phases of the tap sequence, and use the [weapon motion reference workflow](weapon-motion-reference.md) to verify depth and contact before tuning the camera.

Down Left leads with the Quarry Maul's opposite striking face so its windup needs little roll around the shaft. Its `Pose` keys keep the head-centre path and strike markers, and guide the empty swing back to ready. The clip's `PreviewArmRig:left_sleeve_solve_weight` track brings the support sleeve toward its keyed elbow pose through the end of the cut, keeping the cuff away from the lens. Clips without the track use the full procedural solve. Check the third tap in open space through the whole follow-through and beside a pillar through the guided rebound. The [weapon hit shake test](../../tests/weapon_hit_shake.gd) checks windup and rebound roll, follow-through sleeve framing, and the support forearm span.

During a mace light, a fresh LMB press queues one follow-up for **0.50 seconds** by default. Repeated taps refresh that slot. If a tap is still queued when the outgoing `Input Buffer` and `Trace End` have both passed, it starts the next light. A fresh press that is still held can start the next heavy windup at that gate after the usual tap threshold. Releasing that windup early uses the queued light stage. Holding the button through a swing by itself supplies no new queued press. Expired inputs and guard or combat interruptions clear the slot. **Mace Followup Buffer Seconds** in the Player scene's **Abilities** group owns the retention time.

Move the outgoing `Input Buffer` later to add time between swings. `Combo Exit` keeps the outgoing pose in follow-through by the link gate, even when the gate is moved close to the clip end. The incoming `Combo Entry` sets the starting pose for the weapon, arms, and camera blend. It leaves the incoming attack's full clock and trace timing intact. A buffered Right Down → Left uses this same blend and can link on the last tick of the clip. With no accepted follow-up, the mace settles to ready. Test a single too-early tap, repeated taps through the wrap at 1× and raised attack speed, a held follow-up, a late-edited gate, and a buffered light after a hard stop.

The mace clips key local `CameraMotion` rotation from anticipation through follow-through. The rendered weapon sample supplies that rotation alongside procedural effects. Gameplay aim and trace sampling use the gameplay eye. The ready clip supplies a neutral rotation during charge return and hard-stop recovery. Opening a menu after contact freezes the displayed rotation with the weapon, then closing it settles the view as the interrupted attack returns to idle. Hold a fixed aim on a room landmark to verify that the camera returns to neutral after a swing, an interrupted charge, and an interrupted follow-through.

### Two-handed mace contacts

- Each physical contact is ordered by cast time. Same-time terrain precedes the assisted enemy box. Down-left and down-right mace lights lock a visible preferred enemy within **2.85 m**. Its capsule still has to intersect the sweep. After a preferred hit or aim loss, the latest eligible held contact can replay during the next result. Remaining held contacts replay at `Trace End`. Broad attacks resolve contacts directly. Wall cover blocks contact even in locations where the swing carries through a wall.
- [Melee contact tuning](../../data/melee_contact.tres) owns the box width, height, length multiplier, reach target and extension cap, and the fixed view cone. Use **Workbench → Weapons & Player → Melee contact** for a family and weight. The mace's physical `TraceRoot` and `TraceTip` stay on the head while `DamageRoot` and `DamageTip` define the separate damage shaft. Increasing reach assist affects the broad volume and leaves the wall-stop head fit intact.
- Damage and stagger qualify independently against the mass already spent. Cleave power comes from the hand's item-level attack multiplier and the attack's scaling attribute. Each qualifying actor receives its damage and stagger before its mass or target count can end the swing. Shield-like guards add mass, cocoons remain hard stops, armor can stop a light, and charged mace attacks can slide through armor while their budget allows it. Charged mace damage falls off faster than stagger after the opening contact. A stagger-only target can take stun buildup and blunt displacement with its Life preserved. The Thornback's damage and reflection still resolve through its guard ability.
- With the Carry Through practice preset, ordinary solid terrain plays its material impact once per swing and the attack continues. Training lets a valid solid impact stop the swing at ordinary enemy reach. Refuge uses that stop policy with `0.65×` reach and `0.25×` broad width. An ordinary damageable prop shatters and stops the swing. Its `melee_hit_through` flag lets the attack carry through while spending its `melee_contact_mass`. A prop with `allow_melee_damage` disabled stays a solid obstacle.
- The mace's tight head stop uses `Terrain Start`, the visible near face, and a centre-of-cut window within 25° horizontally and 15° above to 28° below gameplay aim. On a charged lateral attack, `Terrain Start` can remember an approaching contact. The cut can stop from `Impact` onward, rechecking that the same surface still blocks the head. Other weapons require physical blade reach and direct aim for an upward-facing surface to stop the swing. Test an angled graze and a rear-face withdrawal as well as a direct pillar hit. The broad damage window still starts at `Trace Start`.
- An accepted hard contact ends the trace and starts the [directional weapon rebound](../../player/mace_rebound.gd). Lateral lights and charged swings on solid stone, metal and wood tuck the shaft across the view from their authored `Impact` pose. The actual contact plane limits the rendered weapon socket's clearance scale around the eye. On charged contacts where the authored head is buried behind that plane, the catch advances immediately to bring it into view on the impact frame. The trace uses the authored scale. Actor stops use the existing direction-specific recoil. Workbench shares the rebound sampler and supplies a preview surface normal.
- The right-to-left charged solid catch angles the shaft upward from the grip and temporarily favors the authored sleeve poses as the hands cross the chest. The [two-hand rig](../../player/weapons/preview_two_hand_rig.gd) still solves toward the wrist markers, and its normal influence resumes as the catch settles. Check the lead sleeve during the first 150 ms after a close pillar hit. Both gloves should follow the shaft and the sleeve should exit below the view without folding over the wrist. The hit-feedback fixture checks the forearm lengths and bend in this pose.
- [Mace camera profiles](../../data/weapon_hit_shake.gd) layer render-only recoil over `CameraMotion`. Charged breakthrough uses its direction-specific kick. A stop takes the higher-priority stop response. A stopped lateral swing turns briefly with the cut before countering and banking. A stopped downcut pitches down before lifting and reversing roll. Material and attack weight set the strength and recovery time. Use a stationary landmark and compare a breakthrough hit, a hard stop, and a whiff. [Reference samples and source limits](vermintide-animation-research.md#calculate-a-hit-and-select-the-response)
- Use **Show Player Weapon Traces** to check a flesh hit, a wall, the metal target, and a breakable prop. Aim straight into the Training Ground pillar at close range and try each light and charged swing. Turn to put the pillar to the side of the main cut and verify that a wide graze carries through. A direct strike catches in view, ends the active trace, and still allows a buffered light to link. Check a light and a heavy into the Armored affix and compare the rebound to a free swing at the same fixed aim. [Vermintide 2 source behavior](vermintide-animation-research.md#calculate-a-hit-and-select-the-response)

## Enemy authorship and AI execution

- Each archetype has a scene, AI script, and motion resource under [combat/enemies](../../combat/enemies/). The scene owns base Life, damage, move speed, and ability references. Its script supplies the definition and enemy-specific choice or execution. [Enemy catalog order](../../combat/enemies/enemy_catalog.gd) is a persistent archetype ID used by the bestiary, saved history, and the Training roster. For a new archetype keep existing order and make the new ID match the appended scene. Derive display data through the catalog and [Bestiary data](../../data/bestiary.gd).
- The shared enemy publishes perception and tactical facts and binds drivers to [`hearth_enemy_tree.tres`](../../ai/hearth_enemy_tree.tres). The runner checks an admitted attack first, then a tactical movement task, patrol, or a wait. Target selection, melee slot ownership, ranged lanes, and attack admission use `AIEncounter3D` from the [native AI addon](../../addons/godot-ai-system/). [Encounter pressure](../../ai/encounter_pressure.gd) applies attack cost multipliers during fade and relax phases. The Training Ground roster selects each archetype, level, and rarity.
- A sight ray against the World layer gates visual aggro and attack checks. Passive discovery has a separate distance condition. Engaged enemies can use remembered contact and navigate to tactical slots. Ranged repositioning follows the movement heading and has a bounded retreat window. A spent retreat can permit a close shot with a clear lane. Keep sight, slot availability, recovery, and attack resources independently testable.
- The attack driver admits one move, enters telegraph, commits, and recovers. The stable ability ID selects the matching `Windup_<id>` and `Strike_<id>` pair when the rig has it. [Enemy model](../../combat/enemies/enemy_model.gd) falls back to the base Windup and Strike clips. Its local bone poses blend locomotion, anticipation, release, and hit reactions. The native driver stretches windup to the actual attack duration and reads the strike trace markers for eligible area attacks. Keep motion changes in the motion resource and retiming in the ability resource.
- Preserve two separate geometry concepts. Start range determines when the actor may commit. Reach and shape determine damage at contact. Ordinary arcs can track during early windup and hold a committed direction at release. Visible ground warnings hold the world footprint from startup. [Telegraph rules](../../data/telegraph_rules.tres) lengthen the warning when the player needs more time to walk out. Use the same origin, direction, and geometry for display, training trace, and hit qualification. Cast times, floor warnings, and status interruptions need an actual gameplay check.
- Keep facing in the same space as the attack. An actor may have a rotated parent. Perception, `_windup_toward`, hit directions, and projectile launch vectors use world space. Capture and update the actor with `global_rotation.y` when turning toward those vectors. Bone channels remain in rig space. The telegraph scene uses a top-level transform to keep its committed footprint independent of later actor movement. [The facing regression](../../tests/enemy_attack_facing.gd) runs the actual models at multiple target bearings and rotated parents.
- Release combat claims and stop obsolete casts when stunned, defeated, or unregistered. Keep transient metamorphosis and revival decisions with their owning archetype. Per-type mechanics such as a cocoon, projectile lob, or sustained beam stay with the owning enemy and its scenes.

## Damage, statuses, and feedback

- [Player attack resolution](../../player/player.gd) composes the equipped hand's base damage, attribute contribution, ability multiplier, critical damage, charge, cleave, added damage types, and active bonuses. [Enemy `take_hit()`](../../combat/enemies/enemy.gd) applies level-gating and target mitigation per damage type and then resolves the combined hit once. Keep displayed damage and granted hit, kill, and status effects tied to the resolved result.
- [Damage resolution](../../combat/damage_resolution.gd) rounds final hits to whole values. Periodic damage retains a rounding remainder per effect so cumulative damage survives tick-size changes. Keep whole applied amount, actual Life lost, and Ward absorption distinct when reporting outcomes. Glancing feedback is a resolved zero-damage contact, and swinging into empty space produces no enemy number. [Combat floaters](../../combat/effects/combat_floaters.gd) display the player's resolved damage to enemies, with distinct emphasis for critical and periodic damage.
- Incoming enemy contact can be evaded or guarded before mitigation and final resource loss. Positive damage consumes Ward before Life. Special knockback and stun take their established live hit paths. Persistent floor effects use `receive_ground_damage()`. [Stun runtime](../../combat/effects/stun_runtime.gd) handles buildup, control loss and recovery. Respect uninterruptible actions when adding buildup or flinch behavior.
- Assign an effect preset to an ability's `on_hit_effects`. It carries chance, duration, potency rules, a stacking policy, icon, and optional applied, looping, and end effects. [Bleed](../../combat/effects/bleed_effect_definition.gd) requires a landed Physical hit and derives its damage from the Physical base before the trigger's critical and target hit defences. Keep recurring visual instances and effect cleanup in [`CombatVisuals`](../../combat/effects/combat_visuals.gd). The HUD gets active statuses from the player and timed card effects. End or clear effects consistently on defeat, cancellation, scene cleanup, and expiry.
- Combat producers emit an event kind and source side with actor, ability ID/name/description, target, and result. Main records order and the [Combat Log](../../ui/combat_log_ui.gd) uses source and event filters and links ability details. Preserve Cast Started → Cast Ended → Damage for a completed cast, or Cast Started → Cast Cancelled when interrupted. A tick from a previously applied effect has its own damage entry.
- [Impact reactions](../../combat/effects/combat_impact.gd) use actual contact coordinates for flesh, stone, and metal. [Camera feedback](../../player/effects/camera/camera_feedback.gd) presents directional damage, vignette, blood, guard, and attack shake independently of physics aim. The [sound catalogue](../../assets/audio/sound_catalogue.tres) is the editable source for cue samples and variations. [Game audio](../../systems/game_audio.gd) resolves cue names.
- Weapon contacts read the [hit shake resource](../../data/weapon_hit_shake.tres) by weapon family, contact, attack weight, and the authored swing used by the striking hand. Where a direction has no override, it inherits the shared profile for that weapon, contact, and attack weight. Tune these profiles in the weapon's **Hit feedback** tab and verify them against a real contact. A profile controls the render camera impulse. Trace termination and the held weapon rebound follow the contact resolver. [Workbench controls](workbench-and-data.md#tune-weapon-hit-shake)
- [Weapon roles](../../combat/weapons/weapon_roles.gd) distinguish edged, blunt, and thrust contact. The configured lethal edged path may sever limbs using the native dismemberment addon through [impact dismemberment](../../combat/effects/impact_dismemberment.gd). Blunt contact influences momentum, with charged blunt hits eligible for live knockback. [Impact tuning](../../data/impact_feedback.tres) controls overkill, resistance, and force. Overkill is resolved damage beyond pre-hit Life. Ragdoll setup runs after deferred severance, uses the remaining mesh, and leaves detached limbs independently simulated. [Enemy ragdoll](../../combat/enemies/enemy_ragdoll.gd) owns body construction. [Combat lifetime rules](../../data/combat_lifetime_rules.tres) author corpse lifetime.
- Standard enemy shots use one [projectile pool](../../combat/effects/enemy_projectile_pool.gd) per floor or training actor container. [Projectile flight](../../combat/effects/enemy_projectile.gd) owns continuous segment collision, final partial distance, VFX fade, and reference cleanup before return. The pool retains a bounded inactive list while active shots live in the actor container. Specialized web, ground, and beam scenes have their own lifecycle.

Keep player level, enemy level, and item level distinct in fixtures and gameplay inputs. Diagnose large claims about balance using reproducible matched-level and level-gap samples before changing multiple tuning resources.

The early [enemy curve](../../combat/enemies/enemy_scaling.tres) gives normal enemies more time to respond. A level-five Grave Thrall has `19.84` Life and `3.0` base attack damage before ability, defense, and encounter modifiers. Early recovery is shorter, and the Training encounter director's relax phase lasts `2.20 s`. Later damage grows more gradually to limit the damage when several enemies connect in the same opening. Abilities still own telegraph duration and movement. The [weapon damage resource](../../data/player_weapon_balance.tres) multiplies the normal two-handed charge bonus by `0.78`. This applies to all damage types on the contact. The two-handed cleave share stays at half for later targets.

Check the normal, same-level case in Training Ground by equipping a Charnel Mallet and setting player and enemy level to five. A plain non-critical charge near the top of its roll should hurt a full-Life Grave Thrall without defeating it outright. Check a moving foe, a guarded hit and an avoidable ground warning separately. The [offense matrix](../../tests/balance_offense_matrix.gd) samples levels 1, 5, 6, 7, 15, and 30 with seeded gear and target ranks. Verify pressure admission in [native AI integration](../../tests/ai_integration.gd).
